D-12
Locked
Assessment throughput and resumability, phone-first — non-negotiable design constraint (restated 25 Aug)
If it is slower than a coach's actual patience, coaches stop, and the whole platform starves. This is the company's fragile link.

Restated 25 Aug. "Under 2 minutes per player" was a per-unit metric on a task nobody does per-unit, and it contradicted D-60's squad-in-under-15-minutes (18 x 2 = 36). The constraint is now: one competency across a squad of 18 in under 90 seconds; any block resumable in 90-second chunks; state saved on every tap; never a session you have to finish. Competency-major sweeps satisfy the original intent at roughly 50 seconds of coach attention per player.

And the harder rule, which outranks both numbers: never ask a coach to enter something he did not already observe. Slow does not kill the record — being asked to rate a child on something you were not watching kills it, because that is the moment an honest coach starts guessing, and a guessing coach makes the whole record worthless. Practically: bands carry forward from the last block by default and the coach touches only what moved; twelve items maximum; no free text required on anybody; targets and notes optional and prompted only on players the coach flagged himself. Asymmetric effort is the design — 45 seconds each for eighteen kids and three minutes each for the two he is actually thinking about.

Design for the sofa, not the carpark. The real assessment moment is 9pm two days after the session, not the touchline. Design for the couch and the carpark comes free; design for the carpark and both are lost.

K · The development framework (D-60)

D-60
Locked
Criterion-referenced competencies on a three-layer framework — clubs bring their own philosophy onto a common spine
No numeric player ratings. Competencies are assessed against the skill itself on developmental bands (Introduced → Developing → Consolidating → Owns it), never against other players. Rationale: relative age and maturation dominate any 1–10 rating of a youth player — a score measures birthdays, not learning — and comparative scoring violates V3. Criterion bands are also faster to complete (buttons, not sliders), defensible to a TD, and portable.

Layer 1 — The Spine (Pitch standard, always assessed): a small set of universal competencies per age stage, organised by the Four Corners (D-13), content aligned to the FA/FFA curriculum stages. This is what travels between clubs (D-48), benchmarks across a league (D-58), and makes "verified on Pitch" mean something. Non-negotiable.
Layer 2 — The Club Layer (club-defined, club-owned): the club adds its own competencies, patterns and values in its own language ("presses using our triggers"). Same bands, same flow. Travels with the record as attributed history ("assessed against Riverside FC's framework"); never presented as cross-club comparable. Capped at ~6–8 items so the assessment still fits a carpark.
Layer 3 — Emphasis: the club flags which spine competencies matter most to how they play — changes ordering and their own dashboard weighting, never the underlying data.

Ownership: the club owns the framework, the coach owns the player. Club-level definition keeps squad data coherent for the TD; coaches customise at player level via 2–3 development targets per block plus notes (the "Story" layer). Age-appropriate content: U8–12 corners hold skill milestones and observed behaviours only — no trait ratings on children, ever; fuller set from ~U13; position-specific competencies from ~U15. Cold start: Pitch ships adoptable templates (curriculum-aligned, possession, participation) for the majority of clubs with no formal framework. Context markers (relative age within band; maturity status later) are recorded so dashboards don't mistake puberty for improvement.

Scope: a minimal club layer (custom items + emphasis, no full builder) ships in the first assessment sprint — without it the first TD demo is us telling them to use our framework. Full framework builder, template library and publishing the framework to the club CV are fast-follows. Still to produce: the actual competency lists per age stage — drafted with design-partner coaches.
