## Why this exists

Pitch holds the football records of children. Most of the adults here are coaches, club officials and parents who have spent years doing this properly, and none of what follows will surprise them.

It is written down anyway, because a platform that trusts everyone to know the rules is a platform that has decided not to have any — and because the one adult who does not already know these things is exactly the person this document is for.

---

## The eight

### 1. You do not contact a child through Pitch

There is no direct messaging here, and there never will be. Do not try to build one out of the parts we give you — a name in a trial application, a club page contact form, a message left in a free-text field for a child to read.

Any approach to a player under 18 from outside their own club goes to that player **and their guardian, together**, and we keep a record of it. Inside a club, coach-to-squad communication runs through the squad and reaches a guardian and a child together.

**Why:** it is the single promise the whole product rests on. A parent approves their child's profile because we told them nobody can reach their child. If that is not true, nothing else here matters.

### 2. You are who you say you are

Your real name. Your real clubs. The badges you actually hold. The role you actually have.

Do not claim to be a coach at a club that has not appointed you. Do not claim to be a technical director — that is granted by a club and confirmed when the club is verified, and it carries club-wide read access to children's records, so it is never something a person types into a form about themselves.

**Why:** verification is not a badge on this platform, it is a gate. It is what unlocks writing to a child's record and seeing players aged 16 and 17. A false claim is not vanity; it is an attempt to get access to children.

### 3. You do not publish other people's children

Do not name, photograph or identify anyone under 18 in content you publish on Pitch — a club page, an alumni list, a trial notice, anywhere.

"A 2019 U13 now in an NPL squad" is a good line and it tells the story. The child's name adds nothing except a searchable identity you do not have permission to publish.

For anyone 18 or over, you need their agreement before you name them.

**Why:** a club administrator typing a child's name is not that child's parent giving consent, and no warranty in a terms page turns it into one.

### 4. Coaches write about football, and only about football

An assessment says what a player can do and what they are working on. That is the whole scope.

Not their body. Not their weight, height or physical maturity as a judgement. Not their family, their finances, or whether their parents turn up. Not their behaviour away from football. Not anything you would not say, in those words, to that child's parent, in front of the child.

**Some words do not exist in this product.** Do not type them:

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| **"struggling"** | A child is not struggling. A child has not got there yet. |
| **"potential"** as a score, rating or indicator | Thirty years of Australian parents have been sold hope with that word. An experienced technical director closes the laptop when they see it. |
| **"elite"** attached to anything below U13 | There is no elite eleven-year-old. There are early developers and late ones. |
| **"talent identification"** applied to a child under 10 | Identifying talent in an eight-year-old is identifying a birthday. |

Write what you saw. Write what is next. That is the job.

### 5. What you learn here stays here

A player's development record exists so that the player, their family and the people coaching them can see it. It is not:

- a scouting list to pass to a mate at another club;
- a way to check on a player before a trial you are not running;
- material for a group chat;
- something to export and keep after you leave.

When you leave a club you keep read access to the assessments **you personally wrote** — your own proof of work — and nothing else. Club-wide access ends the day the role does.

**Why:** a family shares a child's record with a club, not with everyone the club's staff happen to know.

### 6. Nobody here is anybody's agent

Pitch is not an agency and does not want to become one by accident.

Do not use Pitch to solicit players for a paid service, an academy, a showcase, an agent or a trial abroad. Do not offer a family a pathway in exchange for money and do not use a child's record to sell one.

**Why:** the paid-pathway market is where Australian football families get hurt most, and the record we hold would make it easier, not harder. V1 — talent is everywhere, opportunity should be too — means nothing if the record becomes a lead list.

### 7. A club states facts about a coach. It does not publish an opinion about one.

A club can confirm what happened: *that this person coached here, which squads, which seasons, and that they held a current Working With Children Check while they were with us.* Those are attestations a club can stand behind.

**What a club does not do on Pitch is publish an assessment of a coach** — not a paragraph on how they operate, not a rating, not a recommendation, and not a line about whether they would have them back. There is nowhere on Pitch to write one, and that is deliberate.

**Why, and it is the same reason as rule 3.** We refuse on principle to let a club rank a child, or to show a player a club's verdict on them. A reference is that same act pointed at an adult — and unlike a child, an adult has a defamation claim and a livelihood attached to the page. A coaching CV is what a coach sends to their next employer. We are not going to let a club that parted with someone badly write on it.

**Where a coach's credibility comes from instead:** the verified record. Players coached, seasons served, squads held, development delivered. That is worth more to a hiring technical director than a paragraph of prose, and no competitor has it.

### 8. If you see something, report it

Every public page has a report link. You do not need an account, a reason, or certainty.

Report a child's details where they should not be. Report an adult behaving in a way that would worry you at a ground. Report an account that you think belongs to a child pretending to be an adult — **you do not need to be sure, and you will not be told off for being wrong.**

**Why:** the people who know which accounts belong to children are the coaches and parents in that football community. We are not there. You are.

## What happens if you break it

Proportionate, and always with a way back:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Something small or careless** | We tell you, and ask you to fix it. Most of what happens is this. |
| **Something serious, or repeated** | Suspension or closure. We tell you what we have done. |
| **A child's safety** | Immediate action, no notice, and where appropriate a report to police and to the eSafety Commissioner. We may not tell you the reason — explaining it can identify who reported, or teach someone what to do differently next time. |

**You can always appeal**, to [appeals contact], answered within five business days by someone other than whoever made the original decision wherever that is possible. Being unable to be told why is bad enough. Being unable to be heard is not something we are prepared to do to anyone.

## And the part that is not a rule

Most of what makes a club safe for children is not written in a code of conduct. It is the coach who moves a conversation into the open, the team manager who copies a parent in without being asked, the club that has an awkward chat early instead of a serious one later.

Pitch is built so that the safe thing is also the easy thing — the contact routes to the parent, the record cannot be quietly edited, the child's page cannot be found by a stranger. If you ever find that the safe thing here is the hard thing, that is a fault in our design and we would like to know about it. Same report link.
