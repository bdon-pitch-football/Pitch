Pitch 3.0 · Australia

Compliance architecture

Two Australian regimes decide how Pitch must be built for under-16s. Get this right at the design stage and it becomes a moat. Get it wrong and it's existential.

⚠️ Not legal advice. This is research to shape design decisions and to brief a lawyer efficiently. Before launching to a single Australian minor, get a formal opinion from an Australian online-safety / privacy solicitor. The cost of one opinion is trivial next to a $49.5M penalty regime.

① The Social Media Minimum Age regime (the under-16 "ban")

In force · enforcement active 2026

Are we an "age-restricted social media platform"?

A service is caught if social interaction is its sole or significant purpose, users can link to/interact with each other, users can post material, and it's accessible in Australia. Amendment Rules registered March 2026 narrowed this to target services built for persistent engagement — adding a requirement for a recommender feature (algorithmic "for you" curation) or a logged-in feature (endless feeds, feedback mechanisms, time-limited content).

Critically, the law exempts services whose sole or primary purpose is:

  • Professional networking or professional development
  • Supporting user education
  • Facilitating communication between educational institutions and students / their families
  • Messaging, gaming, health, product/service information

Penalties for non-compliance reach $49.5M. Age assurance must be a layered "waterfall" — self-declared age alone is explicitly insufficient.

What would catch us
  • An algorithmic "for you" feed
  • An endless-scroll social feed
  • Likes / comments / reactions between users
  • Open player-to-player following & discovery
  • Positioning Pitch as "LinkedIn for football" in a social sense to minors
What keeps us outside
  • Primary purpose = development tracking & pathway
  • Club-mediated communication only (mirrors the schools exemption almost exactly)
  • No DMs, no open interaction for minors
  • Parent-created, parent-controlled minor accounts
  • Chronological, club-scoped views — no recommender
The design decision this forces
Build two experiences, split at 16

Under 16 — the "development record" experience. No feed, no algorithm, no likes, no comments, no follows, no discovery browsing, no direct contact. A parent-controlled profile, a coach-verified development record, and club-mediated communication. This is a schools-portal shape, not a social-network shape — and it's the shape the exemptions were written for.

16 and over — the full product. The ban applies only to under-16s, so 16–17 year olds can have the network layer, with BUZ's rule intact: contact permission stays parent-mediated until 18, then the account becomes independent.

Neat outcome: this splits exactly where the pricing already splits, and it means the social features that drive growth get built for the audience that's legally allowed to have them.

② The Children's Online Privacy Code

Draft · registration deadline Dec 2026

This one, we don't get an exemption from

The Code applies to services likely to be accessed by children — which Pitch unambiguously is. There's no "we're a sports platform" escape hatch, so it must be built in. Key obligations:

  • Best interests of the child — the governing principle for design decisions
  • Strictly necessary data collection — a higher bar than "reasonably necessary". Collect less than you'd like
  • Parental consent for under-15s, with reasonable steps to verify parental responsibility — not just an email tick
  • Age assurance proportionate to the risk profile of the data held
  • Child-friendly privacy policy, written so a 13-year-old understands it
  • Right of destruction — a child or parent can require deletion of their personal information
  • Privacy impact assessments for high-risk processing, kept in a register
  • Notification where geolocation or parental monitoring is in play

③ What we must build (design requirements)

  • Split the experience at 16 in the data model and the UI from day one — not a later fork.
  • No recommender or endless feed for minors. Chronological, scoped to their own club. This is the single most load-bearing technical decision.
  • No user-to-user interaction primitives for minors — no likes, comments, reactions, follows, DMs.
  • Waterfall age assurance: declared DOB → parent verification (email + separate SMS) → club roster cross-check once clubs are on. Never self-declaration alone. Never government ID.
  • Verify parental responsibility, not just parental identity. The club's registration records are the strongest available proof — build toward club-verified linkage.
  • Data minimisation: if a field isn't strictly necessary for development or safety, don't collect it. No geolocation on minors. No behavioural tracking on minors.
  • One-tap deletion for parent and child, cascading properly through the record.
  • Child-friendly privacy policy plus the adult version.
  • Privacy Impact Assessment before launch, with a maintained register.
  • Working With Children Checks — VIC and NSW require them for anyone working with children. Verify coach WWCC numbers as a platform feature; it protects players, and it's a trust signal to every parent and club.
  • Positioning discipline: describe Pitch as a player development and pathway platform, never as a social network — in the app store listing, the website, and every TikTok caption. How you describe yourself is evidence of purpose.

⑤ Why this is an advantage, not a tax

Every competitor who builds a football app with an open feed and DMs is walking into a $49.5M regime and a privacy code they can't retrofit. Pitch, designed this way from week one, is structurally safe — and that becomes three commercial assets: a sales slide no rival can match in front of a Technical Director who is personally accountable for child safety at their club; the reason parents trust it with their kid's data; and a genuine barrier to any competitor who launched loose and now has to rebuild their foundations to catch up.

⑥ Before launch — the legal to-do list

  • Formal legal opinion on whether the under-16 build falls outside the age-restricted definition. Brief the lawyer with this document — it'll take an hour of their time instead of five.
  • Privacy Impact Assessment + privacy policy (adult and child versions).
  • Terms of service covering minors, parental consent, data rights, and club data-sharing.
  • Data hosting in Australia where practical — expected by clubs and simpler under the Privacy Act.
  • Incident response plan — mandatory notifiable data breach obligations apply.
  • Insurance — cyber and professional indemnity before the first club signs.
