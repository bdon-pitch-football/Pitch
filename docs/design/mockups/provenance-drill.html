Pitch · design proposal · 24 Sep 2026 · mockup 1 of 5 · the one that matters most in a year

The provenance drill

A stat on a CV opens, in place, into where that number came from. Two levels, pushed not overlaid. It is the only move in this set that makes Pitch structurally different from a profile page, and D-62 already forces the data onto every stat — we render it as a 9px chip.

What is wrong now

components/cv/PlayerCV.tsx:43 renders the whole of D-62 as a pill reading Self-reported, 9px, 65% white, once, above the row. Below it three tiles carry a number each and nothing else. So the most important claim on the page — who says so — is the smallest type on the screen, and it is attached to the block rather than to the number. A technical director reading eleven goals has no way to ask the page where eleven came from, and the page has no answer even though the database holds one on every row. Under the number there is nothing. That is the shape of a social profile, which gets thinner the deeper you go.

What the change does for the person on this screen

Two different people, one mechanic.

The club. A TD working a register opens the tile and gets the date it was entered, the season, and the squad it belongs to. When assessments ship, the same tile says a coach's name, their club and the date they confirmed it — and that is the moment the CV stops being a claim and starts being a record. Nothing else we could build changes a TD's trust in this page as much.

The fourteen-year-old. Depth that rewards looking is the opposite of a number that invites inflating. The page is visibly a ledger with dates on it, not a scoreboard. There is no ranking, no comparison, no other child anywhere in the mechanic.

What it costs to build

Low, and no schema at all. player_stat is already keyed (record_id, season, stat_key, source_experience_id) and carries provenance; the row's own created_at and the squad are already joined onto the page. The work is one component — StatTile becomes a disclosure — plus the two or three lines it renders. Production form is <details name="stat">, the native exclusive accordion: no JavaScript, no state, one open at a time, and it keeps working with the bundle stalled. This mockup uses radio inputs only because the well has to sit outside the three-column grid.

The one real cost is a permission line, not a day of work. Everything the tile reveals must come back through the single tokenised read path (D-80) — not a second route, not a client fetch. That is an hour if it is done on the first day and a rewrite if it is not.

What it does NOT change

Not the closed tile: collapsed, the row is pixel-identical to what ships today — same 21px numeral, same 10.5px label, same 7px gap, same three tiles. Not the never-zero rule (D-70): a tile that is not rendered has nothing to open. Not the stat catalogue. Not the order of anything on the page. Not the 9.5px Self-reported pill, which stays where it is as the summary of the block. No modal, ever: a modal over a child's CV is a second render of the most dangerous surface we have.

The decision this needs from BUZ, and it is one line

At level two, does a stranger holding the link see the coach's name? A verified club should. A person who was forwarded a fourteen-year-old's CV in a WhatsApp group is a different reader, and naming an adult who coaches that child to that reader is a contact route we did not mean to give. The mockup draws the restrictive answer — link-holders get "Confirmed by a coach at Riverside FC", verified clubs get the name — because CLAUDE.md says to choose the more restrictive option and tell you what was chosen. It is a doc 14 question, not a design one.

Two things this panel is doing deliberately
One. The third tile is still self-reported and still opens to the self-reported well. A page with mixed provenance must look normal, not broken — it is the state almost every real CV will be in for years, and a design that only looks right when every stat is verified is a design that never looks right.
Two. A verified club reads the line above with the coach’s name on it. A stranger holding the share link reads “Confirmed by a coach at Riverside FC · 12 September 2026” — the same fact, no adult named to an unknown reader. One boolean from the permission function decides which line renders, and which one is right is BUZ’s call, not mine.
