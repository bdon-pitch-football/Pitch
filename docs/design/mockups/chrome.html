The chrome, made consistent

Four things in the product's chrome are inconsistent in ways a technical director notices in the first ten seconds, and none of them is a redesign. This sheet shows them next to each other, because the argument is visual and a list of them reads like nitpicking.

Nothing here changes what is on a screen, in what order, or in what words. Every fix below is the container (D-147). At 390 every one of them is invisible today and stays invisible after — all four defects only exist because a laptop has room for containers of different widths.

D · Three desktop layouts where D-147 declares two
"Two desktop layouts, no more… that is what makes this two desktop designs rather than seventy-five."
1 · Reading — 640px centred · public pages, auth, /report, /trials, /g/* · declared
2 · Console wide — 232px sidebar + 932px · /club/register, /club/squads, /club/roles, /ops/verification · declared
3 · Console + rail — 232px sidebar + ~594px + 320px rail · player, coach and club /home, /ops/reports · undeclared
The third one is the reason the dashboards feel like a different product from the console. It is also genuinely the best screen we have — player /home is layout 3 and it is one of the three screens the audit calls finished work. So the answer is not to delete it.
decision for BUZ
Name the third layout, and give it a rule that stops it spreading
See decision D-c3 in the report. The rule I would write:
Layout 3 — console with an accountability rail. A console surface may carry a 320px right rail, and the rail may hold only a block that states what has happened to this person's or this club's data — who read it, what is live, what expires, what is waiting. It may never hold navigation that the sidebar already holds, and it may never hold a suggestion.

The test: if the block would be a lie tomorrow because nobody did anything, it belongs in the rail. If it would say the same thing forever, it is furniture and it goes in the sidebar or nowhere.

The parity rule: a rail may only hold a block that also exists on the 390px artboard. If it is not on the phone, it is not allowed on the laptop (D-147 constraint 3).
Under that rule, player /home's "Who has read your registrations" stays exactly as it is; club /home's six duplicated nav buttons fail it and are replaced (mockup 4); and /ops/reports, which today puts the whole page's weight in the 320px rail, fails it backwards and is inverted (mockup 3).
