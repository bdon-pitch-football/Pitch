Pitch · design proposal · 23 Sep 2026 · mockup 5 of 7

The two empty screens

Both are 70% empty at 1280 and 73–74% at 820, and they are empty for opposite reasons. /jobs is short — a real list with two things in it. A brand-new /home is first-use — a real account with nothing in it yet. Every product looks broken at these two moments, which is why they are worth the same care as the screens that carry data.

One rule runs through both, and it is Carbon's taxonomy in our words: an empty screen must say what will appear here, when, and the one thing worth doing now. Never an illustration — we have a pitch mark and a gradient — and never an apology.

1 · /jobs — the coaching board

What is wrong now

The screen is finished by y=540: a title, a three-numeral stat row, two role cards each carrying three short lines, and a note. The stat row is naked on the background (the same component appears in a gradient hero on the club dashboards and in a plain card on the parent's home — three clothes, one component). The role cards are the thinnest thing on the page and they are the page's whole point.

What the change does for the coach

The board is grouped by when the role closes, which is the only ordering a coach hiring season has, and each row carries what a coach decides on: the club and whether it is verified, the age group, the commitment, whether it is paid, when it was posted and when it closes. Two roles under two dated headers read as a board with two things on it. Two cards under a bare title read as a stub. This is the same move that makes /club/register the best screen in the product — it groups by squad, and a short register still looks organised.

Underneath, one honest sentence about the slice we can actually fill (D-74's rule, applied to the jobs board) and the note about what applying sends, kept verbatim.

Cost

Low. A sort and a header, plus three fields already on the coaching_role row. No new query.

What it does NOT change

Not the order of the board — still chronological, still nothing ranked or recommended (D-74's rule for the trials index applies here for the same reason). Not the reading column: this stays a reading surface and does not get a rail. Not the copy of the applying note.

2 · A brand-new /home

What is wrong now

This is the first screen a real user sees, and the code's own comment says it used to be a dead end. It is better than that now — three doors that work — but it is still three 60px cards and a grey paragraph that ends: "Adding a child, claiming a club page and building a player CV are not on this screen yet — tell us which you came for and we will point you at it." It asks the person to tell us and then gives them nothing to tell us with. That is the single most fixable line in the product.

What the change does for the person

Three things. The doors become rows with a sentence each, so a person who has just signed up can tell which one is theirs. The dead sentence gets a real route — the support address, which exists. And the fold is filled with what is true about their account right now, in the product's own voice: Australia only at launch, nothing about a child exists until a parent has approved it, and one tap deletes everything. A new account has no data, but it is not without facts.

Cost

Very low. No new query at all — this branch runs when children.length === 0. It is markup and copy.

What it does NOT change

No new capability, no onboarding wizard, no role-picking step that would fork signup. The three doors are the same three pages. The "not on this screen yet" admission stays — it is honest and it is the reason this screen exists at all.

All new copy on both screens goes past the copy check before it ships, including the D-85 banned-words pass.
