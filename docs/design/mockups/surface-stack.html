Pitch · design proposal · 24 Sep 2026 · mockup 5 of 5 · the one that changes every screen at once

The surface stack, made perceptible

Five named surface levels ship. A card sits on the page at 1.079 : 1 and a sunken well sits inside a card at 1.038 : 1, where 1.00 is identical. The whole system — page, sunken, card, control, border — lives between green channel 11 and 36 out of 255. This page shows the figures under the swatches, and then the same real screen drawn on both ladders, so the ratio is something you can see rather than something you have to believe.

What is wrong now

The product reads as a wireframe that got coloured in. The 1px border is doing all the separating, because there is nothing else: 12 box-shadow declarations in the whole of app/ and not one is a resting elevation on a card — the only real depth in the product is .lift:hover, a shadow that exists only under a mouse pointer and therefore never renders on a phone at all.

--surface-sunken is the sharpest version of the problem. It was added deliberately, with a comment saying two levels forced every card to look identical — and then given a value 1.038 from the thing it is meant to sit below. The diagnosis was right and the number cannot be seen. That is also why .card-sunken has barely been adopted: it looks like nothing, so nobody reaches for it.

And globals.css:6 sets body to #070b09, which is not --bg. There is an unnamed sixth level under everything and --bg is itself a raised surface. Nobody decided that.

What the change does

Four values move. The page colour does not. Every step becomes 1.13 : 1, which puts a card at 1.27 : 1 against the page — inside the band Carbon ships for its dark themes (Gray100 → Gray90 is 1.20, Gray90 → Gray80 is 1.31) and far enough apart to read as an object without becoming grey. --line moves with them so the hairline keeps its job.

And the rule that comes with it, which is worth more than the values: depth into the record goes down, action goes up. Raised is where you act; sunken is what you read; every level of disclosure renders sunken inside its parent. That is what makes the provenance drill (mockup 1) read as being inside the stat block without a single shadow, and it is why this mockup is last in the set but first in dependency order.

Three consequences, stated plainly because they are the cost

1 · --muted has to move too. Lifting the card lowers the contrast of grey text on it: #7d8f85 drops from 5.14 to 4.35 : 1 on the new card, which is below AA for small text. #8a9d92 restores it — 5.19 on the card, 4.58 on --surface-2, 6.61 on the page. That is a fifth token, and it is not optional; a contrast failure in exchange for a nicer-looking card is not a trade we take.

2 · body and --bg must be reconciled (globals.css:6). One line, and it removes the phantom level.

3 · --hero will sit below the card and look wrong. The gradient's middle stop #0c1d14 is 1.09 against the page; the new card is 1.27. A hero that is darker than the cards under it inverts the whole stack. The hero is named in the charter, so I have not touched it — but this change forces the question, and the honest answer is that --hero needs re-pitching in the same pass. That is a decision for BUZ, and it is the reason this is a proposal and not a patch.

What it does NOT change

Dark only. The accent, on-accent, amber, purple and red are untouched. The type scale, the five letter-spacings, the four weights, the radii, the two button heights, the 44px minimum, the logo placement: all untouched. No shadow is added anywhere — the hairline plus a real surface step is the elevation, which is the published guidance for dark UI and costs nothing to render. And it adds no level: still five, with the same names, so every component that already says var(--surface) gets the change for free and every component still carrying its own hex does not — which is the adoption argument all over again.

The honest limit of this proposal. Moving the tokens only changes the screens that use the tokens. Token adoption across the product is roughly one in eight — most components still carry T.surface from lib/palette.ts as an inline hex, and palette-check.mjs asserts the two sets are identical, so changing one without the other fails CI. This change is therefore a two-part job: move both sets in the same commit, then keep moving components onto var(--token) screen by screen. The second half is where the real work is, and it is worth doing whether or not the values change — a token set copied into two dozen files is a fork that has not happened yet.
