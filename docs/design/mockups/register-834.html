Pitch · design proposal · 24 Sep 2026 · mockup 4 of 5 · the one I would build first

The register on an iPad in portrait

Every iPad in portrait except the 12.9" gets the phone layout: one hundred families as one hundred stacked cards, 17,347 pixels of scroll at 820 against 9,645 at 1280. And the 12.9", which does reach the console, is the one width where the table overflows the screen by 7px. The person this happens to is the only person who pays us.

What is wrong now, in two parts

Part one — the cliff at 1024. globals.css:195 and :533 put the console behind @media (min-width: 1024px). An iPad Pro 12.9" in portrait is exactly 1024 CSS px, so it lands on the first pixel of the desktop design with none of the room it assumes. The evidence set caught it: /club/register reports overflowPx: 7 at 1024 and 0 at 1280, and the overflowing elements are named — DIV.console-row console-head and every DIV.console-row console-row-hover under it. The arithmetic is the whole story:

Treat that arithmetic as the mechanism, not the measurement. My sums say the row is about 20px too wide; the instrumented figure is 7px, so one of the paddings above is not what I have assumed — whoever fixes this should measure it in the browser rather than trust my column. What is not in doubt is the shape: a grid with hard minimums does not fold, it spills, and the minimums add up to more than a 232px sidebar leaves behind at 1024. It resolves at about 1031, which is why it is invisible on every laptop anyone here owns and present on the one device a technical director actually carries to a ground.

Part two, and it is the expensive one — every other iPad gets the phone. 768, 810, 820 and 834 all fall below the breakpoint, so the paid surface renders as stacked cards: a card per family, each with its own buttons. At 820 the page is 17,347px tall. The same register at 1280 is 9,645px. A TD working a hundred families on an iPad reads one clipped card at a time and scrolls the height of a forty-storey building to do it.

What the change does for the person on this screen

It separates two things D-147 currently says in one breath: "sidebar instead of tab bar" and "table instead of cards". They do not need the same breakpoint. Below 1024 the club seat already carries the phone tab bar, so navigation is solved at 834 without a sidebar — which frees the entire 834 for the table. The table appears from 768; the sidebar still appears at 1024. Two media queries instead of one, and the iPad gets the console it has room for.

The row also loses 30px of column minimum in the process, which is what fixes the 7px overflow at 1024 — the same change repairs both halves.

What it costs to build

Very low — it is two numbers and five column widths. .d-only and .m-only move to a min-width: 768px query; the .console-nav sidebar block stays at 1024. Then globals.css:214's grid-template-columns becomes the set below, which fits at 768 and every width above it:

Nothing else is touched. No new capability appears at any width (D-147 constraint 3): the same rows, the same actions, the same permissions, the same copy.

What it does NOT change

Not the phone. Below 768 the cards stay exactly as drawn. Not the content or the order — the header, the four numerals, the parent-approved reassurance line, the three filter rows and the squad grouping are identical. Not the type scale, at any width. Not the 44px minimum on any target: every control on the 834 table is 44px tall, which is why the row is 70px and not 44. And no search box has been drawn here even though the register badly needs one — that is a new capability and a new input, and it belongs in its own decision, not smuggled in with a breakpoint fix.

This is a change to D-147, so it is BUZ's, not mine

D-147 says "≥1024px — console surfaces" and "640–1023px: content column caps at 560px". Moving the table to 768 contradicts the second line. What it buys: the paying seat becomes usable on the device most club officials own, and the 7px overflow disappears. What it costs: one more layout to check on every console screen — six screens, five widths — and the 560px cap now has an exception that has to be written down or someone will "fix" it back. What it does not cost: a forked design system. It is still two desktop layouts; one of them simply starts earlier.

What I did not draw, and would not smuggle in here. A search box — the register has zero text inputs and a hundred first names, two of them identical, and that is a real defect, but it is a new capability on a surface full of children and it deserves its own decision. Sorting. A club-side note field. Returning to scroll position after opening a CV — which is the single most expensive thing on this screen and is a routing fix, not a layout one. All four are in the journeys report. None of them belongs in a breakpoint change.
