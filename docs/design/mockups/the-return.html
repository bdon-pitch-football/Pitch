The return

Our user is gone from March to September and that is normal, not failure. The screen a returning family lands on should lead with what happened while they were away — facts, dated, with no verb aimed at the reader — and then get out of the way.

What is wrong now

Nothing on /home knows how long you have been gone. A parent who last opened Pitch in July gets the identical page to one who opened it yesterday: three numerals, then a queue of things waiting on them, the oldest of which has been waiting since July and says so in grey 11px type. The first thing a returning parent reads is a list of their own omissions. Nothing on the page tells them what happened to their child's record in the eleven weeks they were away — even though we hold all of it: an append-only read ledger (D-78), the share-token expiry (D-53), the consent timeline, and a curated trials board.

What the change does for the person on this screen

It answers the question a parent actually returns with — "did anything happen?" — before it asks them for anything. Four dated lines, three of which are reassurance and one of which is a calendar fact. The read line is the strongest thing we own: a named person at a named club opened your child's CV on a date, and we can prove it. No competitor can print that sentence honestly.

The last line is the seasonality move, and it is stated as a limit, not an opportunity. Football Victoria's trial windows exist so that adults cannot approach children out of season. We display the window. We never say "clubs are looking — update your CV now".

What it costs to build

The cheapest thing in this set. Half a day. One comparison — last_seen_at against 60 days — and four queries the product already runs somewhere: fn_register_readers (the ledger on this page's own child cards), the live share_token expiry (already rendered as "expires 12 October"), a count of outside-approach events on the consent spine, and the next trial window from the curated index. No new table, no new permission, no new screen.

What it does NOT change

It is rendered on arrival and never pushed. No email, no SMS, no push, no badge — the moment this becomes a send it is a re-engagement prompt, which is banned outright over a minor (D-65 as amended by D-81) and is a marketing send to a guardian, which launch does not have. There is no streak, no count of visits, no "you haven't updated in six months", no completeness score, no comparison to another family, and nothing on it is aimed at bringing a child back. It disappears entirely for anyone who was here last week. The rest of the page — the hero numerals, the waiting queue, oldest first, the children cards — is untouched and in the same order.

Copy rule: underlined = suggested, not approved. Everything else is verbatim from app/home/page.tsx or components/RegisterReaders.tsx. The guardian’s own name is underlined because there is no guardian in lib/fixtures.ts — the one seat with no house fixture is the one that holds every consent decision in the product. That is worth fixing before it is worth drawing.

Read the four lines again and count the verbs aimed at the reader. There are none. Nothing says update, renew, check, complete or act. Two of the four are the absence of something happening, which is the answer a parent wants most and the one no engagement-shaped product would ever print. The expiry line states a date and a consequence, not an instruction — the renewal control is where it has always been, on the child's card below.
