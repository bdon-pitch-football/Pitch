/club/billing

The emptiest screen in the product — 80% at 1280, 74% at 820 — and it is the page attached to $54 a month. A treasurer opens it to check what the club is on and finds four elements finished by y=330 with 470px of background under them and 386px beside them.

What is wrong now

It is a named console surface in D-147 (ClubBilling) rendering as a 604px reading column. It shows the price and nothing else: no next charge, no card, no receipt, no record of what has been paid, and no answer to the only question a treasurer asks a subscription page — what is this buying us? It also carries a "< Back" link above a page whose sidebar item is already highlighted, which is two navigation systems on one screen.

What the change does for the person on this screen

It moves to the width D-147 already gives it and fills the space with facts about this club's money and this club's register — the next charge, the statement descriptor, the receipt address, and what the register has done since the last one. A treasurer can answer "what is PITCH FOOTBALL on our statement" without ringing the bank, which is the thing D-136 was written to prevent. And the cancel route stays exactly where D-136 requires it: here, in the club's own settings.

What it costs to build

Low for the left column and the rail — the plan, the renewal date, the descriptor and the cancel route are already on the page or already in the record; the layout is the console grid that /club/register already uses. Moderate for "What you have paid": charges are not stored today, only subscription state is, so that block needs an invoice.paid webhook writing a row. I would ship the page without it and add it when a club asks for a receipt.

What it does NOT change

Not the price, not the plans, not one word of the D-136 disclosure or the D-137 authority tick. Not the checkout flow. Not the unpaid state's content or order — that form is already good and only gains the second column. Not the phone: at 390 this is the same screen, in the same order, with the rail's blocks stacked under the main column exactly as the 390px artboard has them.
