Pitch · design proposal · 23 Sep 2026 · mockup 3 of 7

/ops/reports

72% empty at 1280, and the page's entire weight is in its margin. The 594px main column holds one card reading "No open reports." and then 600px of nothing; the 320px rail carries three sections of dense copy, a held-accounts panel, a search field and the 1800RESPECT guidance. A stranger would assume the page had half-loaded.

What is wrong now

The columns are the wrong way round. This is the report and takedown desk — the screen that proves to a regulator, a journalist or a parent that a person reads every report — and it is drawn as though the queue were an afterthought. It also breaks the rail rule the rest of this proposal asks for: a rail holds state you read, and this rail holds three tools you work, one of which ends in a court-order field.

What the change does for the person on this screen

The queue becomes the page. Each report is a row in the desk's own table — concern, what it points at, when it arrived, and the one action that moves it — at the density /ops/verification already uses, which the audit calls the tightest table we have. The invariant goes above the data, as it does on verification. "One parent's access" moves out of the rail and into the main column as what it is: a deliberate, slow, separate piece of work. And the rail holds the two things that are genuinely standing state — what is hidden right now, and who is held at signup.

The empty state is the half that matters most, because the desk is empty most days. Today it says "No open reports." and looks broken. An empty queue should be evidence, not absence: nothing open, what was closed recently, and how long the oldest one waited.

What it costs to build

Low. Every query on this page already exists; two of them move between columns. The queue row is the .console-row grid that /club/register and /ops/verification already ship. The only new query is the closed-report count for the empty state — one count(*) over report where actioned_at is not null.

What it does NOT change

Nothing about what an operator can do, or to whom. D-79 holds untouched: nothing here opens a child's record, a player page is still identified by its link's fingerprint and a family by a first name. No copy is softened — the 1800RESPECT line, the "suppress first" instruction and the court-order requirement are quoted verbatim from the page as built. No new action, no new permission, no bulk anything.
