Pitch · design proposal · 23 Sep 2026 · mockup 6 of 7

/club/squads and /club/squads/<id>

These two screens use the width and not the density. D-147 says console surfaces get "tables not stacked cards". /club/register and /ops/verification are real tables and they are the two best screens we have. Squads is eleven bordered cards at about 105px each — 1,150px of scroll for eleven rows that carry a name, a meta line, a link and a grey label — and the detail page is the same again, with roughly 700px of each 932px row empty.

What is wrong now, in order of how much it costs the TD

1. The creation form sits above the list. A technical director running eleven squads scrolls past an "Add a squad" form every single time he comes to look at them. He adds a squad about twice a season.
2. Eleven cards where a table belongs. Nothing lines up down the page, so comparing two squads means reading two paragraphs.
3. The right edge is ragged. "In use — can't be removed" wraps to two lines and sits on a different baseline from "Who plays", so no two rows end in the same place.
4. A 900px-wide accent "Add it" button — a phone control scaled up, because the charter fixes button height and says nothing about width.
5. On the detail page, "Ask them" is an accent-outlined button — the fourth visual language for an action, on the only screen in the product that has it.

What the change does for the technical director

The register's own treatment, applied to squads: group headers by stage with counts, a sticky column head, hairline rows, one strong left-aligned first column, and the counts the page already queries — registered and playing — as their own numeric columns instead of buried in a grey sentence. Eleven squads become one card you can read down in about two seconds. The creation form stays exactly where it is in the order (D-147: the artboard decides order) but collapses behind a 44px "Add a squad" chip, the same <details> disclosure the trials board already ships — 44px instead of about 330px, at both widths.

On the detail page the same treatment turns a stack of 85px cards into a squad sheet a coach can scan: player, positions in pick order, clips, this season's surfaced stats, and one action in the same place on every row.

What it costs to build

Low, and it has a precedent. The .console-row grid, the .console-head sticky head, the .console-row-hover and the .d-only / .m-only pair all exist and are used by /club/register today. Below 1024px the squads stay as cards, exactly as the register's rows already do. The columns are all fields the page already selects.

What it does NOT change

No squad data changes, no new field, no new query. The order of the page is unchanged — the form is still first, just closed. Row height stays at 52px with a 44px touch target inside it; we do not shrink a row to fit more on a screen (D-147 constraint 4). And the never-zero instinct holds: a squad with nobody registered shows a dash, not a nought — the same rule as D-70's stat tiles, applied to a table column.
