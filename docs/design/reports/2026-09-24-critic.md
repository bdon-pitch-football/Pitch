# critic: where Pitch is just another app (24 Sep 2026)

**Asked:** BUZ wants the product to be "interactive, have layers, and not just be another app."
Find where it is not, with receipts. Read-only; no product code or copy changed.

**Method:** read `CLAUDE.md` (Night Match charter) and `docs/team/TRAINING.md`; minted seat
cookies the way `scripts/screens.mjs:33` does and used the running app on :3000 as
signed-out, player, parent, coach and club-TD; read the 1280 captures in
`docs/design/screens/`; read source for every claim below.

**Two caveats, stated up front.**
1. The shared dev database is being mutated while I worked — Deniz's stats read 16/8 in the
   captured PNG, 9 live, then 18/11/7 ten minutes later. The captures and the live app
   disagree. Nothing below depends on a stat value.
2. **I withdrew a finding.** I first reported the public CV's stat row as broken — one grey
   tile in a four-wide row. It is not. I had screenshotted it mid-entrance-animation.
   Measured live: `repeat(3, minmax(0,1fr))`, 484px of a 524px parent, opacity 1, correct
   tokens. The CV renders correctly. I am recording the miss because a critic seat that
   only reports hits is not being audited.

---

## The short version

The product is not generic everywhere. Three screens could not be anything but this, and one
of them is very good. But the app has **one axis of depth it has fully built and never
rendered — time** — and it has confused "no social features" with "no life". The result is a
product that is finished the week you finish your CV.

Ranked by severity.

---

## 1 · The most alive thing we have built is the page for people who are not users yet

**Severity: 1. This is the finding.**

`components/coming-soon/ComingSoon.tsx` is **1,404 lines** and renders at `/`
(`app/page.tsx:13`). It contains: a drag-and-shoot ball with a pull-back aim line and
GOAL/POST/WIDE/SHORT verdicts, drag-to-move tactics magnets, draw-a-run-with-your-finger on a
pitch, a formation picker that re-lays the magnets, a persona switcher wired to a ground plan,
slide-to-approve, hold-to-pause with a filling bar, a highlights carousel, an FAQ accordion and
a scroll-progress bar.

The signed-in product contains **six** client components with three distinct behaviours:

| Behaviour | Where |
|---|---|
| A progress bar that moves as you type | `app/build/[recordId]/BuildForm.tsx:40–68` |
| A number that counts up on load | `components/cv/StatTile.tsx:30–47` |
| Copy→"Copied", click-to-play façade | `components/cv/CopyLink.tsx`, `components/cv/ClipCard.tsx` |

For comparison: `PlayerCV.tsx` is 288 lines, `BuildForm.tsx` is 198. The marketing page is
roughly five times the size of the product's flagship artefact.

Everything else in the app is a server form: **99** `<form action={serverAction}>` instances,
and **zero** uses of `useOptimistic`, `useTransition`, `useFormStatus`, `useActionState` or
`revalidatePath` — that count is literally zero, not "few". Feedback is always a full
round-trip to `?done=` and a `role="status"` banner. The only "submitting" state in the entire
product is the word `Uploading…` at `BuildForm.tsx:114`.

**Why this matters more than it reads.** A visitor's first experience of Pitch is the most
interactive it will ever be. Signing up is a downgrade. We are advertising a product that is
more alive than the product.

## 2 · The development record — the thing we say is the product — has no screen

**Severity: 1.**

The mission line is "a living record of their development." The schema honours it completely.
`supabase/migrations/0002_phase1_core.sql:231–235` creates `record_entry` with exactly the six
types the spec names — `attendance`, `participation`, `effort_observation`, `milestone`,
`coach_note`, `competency_observation` — plus `assessment_entry` with `supersedes_entry_id`,
`assessment_session`, `competency`, `growth_note`, `match_appearance`, and the 48-hour
edit-window triggers in `0015_write_permissions.sql`.

I grepped `app/`, `lib/` and `components/` for all of them myself. **Zero hits.** Not one line
of application code reads or writes any of it.

What a family actually sees is `lib/record-read.ts:59–120` → `components/cv/PlayerCV.tsx`:
positions, squad number, foot, about, stats, achievements, history, other football. A CV the
player typed in. Plus, for the parent only, a consent log.

This is the documentation-versus-screen gap the brief asked me to look for, and it is the
widest one in the product. **On screen, the "living record" is a form you filled in once.**
I accept this is a deliberate Stage-2 deferral (`0015_write_permissions.sql:6` says so
outright). The finding is not that we broke a rule — it is that the sentence we lead with
describes a screen that does not exist, and everything in §3 follows from that.

One live consequence today, not December: `lib/messages.ts:613` tells a family on deletion
that *"any coach who wrote an assessment keeps an anonymous count."* No coach can write one.

## 3 · Why someone leaves: nothing comes back

**Severity: 1. The honest answer to "what brings them back in week six" is: nothing.**

- **No in-app notification, inbox, badge or "what's new" anywhere.** Grep-confirmed across
  `app/`, `components/`, `lib/`. Every one of the ~40 messages in `lib/messages.ts` is email or
  SMS. The only reader of `message_outbox` in a page is `app/dev/outbox/page.tsx`, which
  `notFound()`s outside dev.
- **The only autonomous machinery deletes.** `vercel.json` registers three crons.
  `app/api/jobs/daily/route.ts:18–45` is `fn_purge_pending`, `fn_purge_cancelled_registers`,
  `fn_purge_past_trials`, `fn_lapse_send_requests`, `fn_lapse_interest_requests`,
  `fn_lapse_squad_invitations`, `fn_purge_abuse_signals`. The one thing it sends that a player
  would want — the 16th-birthday message — is in `HELD_KEYS` (`lib/messages.ts:824`) and sends
  nothing today. `/api/digest` goes to the founder.
- **Nothing is comparable to anything.** Stats are one value per key, overwritten on save
  (`BuildForm.tsx:14`). There is no season dimension in the UI, no prior season, no delta, no
  trend, no arrow. The tagline at `app/page.tsx:7` is **"every season on the record"** and the
  product renders exactly one.
- **108 inline SVGs in `app/` + `components/`. Zero plot data.** Nothing anywhere computes an
  SVG coordinate, length or path from a stat or a date. `PlayerCV.tsx` — a player's whole
  season on one page — contains four SVGs, all icons.

**The picture of this finding is the coach's home screen**
(`docs/design/screens/1280-coach-home.png`): "YOUR PAGE — **6 of 6 done**", a full green bar,
"Every part of your page is filled in", one link — and then roughly 450px of empty background
to the bottom of a 1280×800 screen. *The product shows you less the more complete you are.*
Completion is rewarded with an empty room.

**And we have already built the cure and buried it.** `components/RegisterReaders.tsx` renders
*"Marina Petrovic · Technical director · Opened the CV · last 23 Sep 2026"* — a named adult,
a real action, a date, with "Take off this register" beside it. That is genuine consequence,
it needs no feed and no social graph, and it is the single best reason-to-return in the
codebase. It is a below-the-fold block on `/home` for 16+ only
(`app/home/page.tsx:641–645`). It has no screen of its own.

## 4 · The paid core is an applicant tracking system wearing different words

**Severity: 2.**

`/club/register` (`docs/design/screens/1280-club-td-club_register.png`) is the screen the
money is attached to. On it: a KPI row (**100** PLAYERS · **79** NEW · **11** SHORTLISTED ·
**10** INVITED), three rows of faceted filter chips with counts, then a table with columns
PLAYER / THEIR LINE / STATUS, a status pill per row, "Open the CV", and a primary action
button — "Invite to trial".

D-108 is emphatic that *application, applied, declined, rejected, unsuccessful* appear nowhere,
and the strings comply. But **the vocabulary was changed and the interaction model was not.**
Strip the logo and the word football and this is Greenhouse, Lever or Workable: a funnel with
stage counts, a filterable candidate list and a per-candidate advance button. NEW →
SHORTLISTED → INVITED *is* a hiring pipeline. A TD will call it a shortlist because it looks
exactly like one, whatever we write on it.

Two specifics:

- **The status palette is off-charter and it is what makes the screen read generic at a
  glance.** `app/home/page.tsx:292–293` uses `T.amber` and `T.purple`. The Night Match charter
  grants ten colours plus the hero gradient; amber and purple are not among them.
  Green/amber/purple stage pills are the CRM palette — it is the most recognisable visual
  signature in B2B SaaS, and we are wearing it on the one screen clubs pay for.
- **The one element that could not appear in a hiring tool is the narrowest column.**
  "THEIR LINE" — the child's own words, in quotes: *"Plays in front of the back four. Two
  seasons at this level."* That is the whole argument for this product being different, and it
  is sandwiched between a name column and a status pill.

The banner above the table — *"Every under-16 here was put on this register by a parent"* —
is excellent and also could not be anywhere else. The screen has the right ingredients in the
wrong proportion.

**Related, same screen family:** the TD console's right-hand rail is its left-hand navigation
printed twice. Left nav: Home, Register, Squads, Crest & club page, Coaching roles, Post a
trial, Your club page, Plan & billing. Right rail: the same seven, minus Home, reordered. This
is deliberate — `components/console-shell.tsx:8–15` states the rule, *"THE SIDEBAR IS A SECOND
WAY TO THE SAME DOORS, NEVER A NEW DOOR"* — and the rule is sound. The outcome at 1280 is not:
the densest, highest-value seat in the product spends a third of its screen on a duplicate
menu. The rule says the sidebar may not invent doors; it does not say the content column must
give up its right third to repeat them.

## 5 · The first screen a real user sees is the weakest screen in the product, and it apologises

**Severity: 2.**

`docs/design/screens/1280-brand-new-home.png`, rendered by `app/home/page.tsx:660–697`.

A brand-new account gets no frame, no nav rail and no tab bar — `PlayerFrame` and
`GuardianFrame` are never invoked on this branch. Just a title and three rows with Start /
Open links. Strip the logo and it is any B2B onboarding checklist ever shipped.

Then, verbatim on screen:

> "Adding a child, claiming a club page and building a player CV are not on this screen yet —
> tell us which you came for and we will point you at it."

Three of the four things a person could plausibly have signed up to do, listed as things this
screen cannot do. The only "Start" offered is **Build a coach CV** — so the empty state
assumes you are a coach.

The code comment above it is candid (`app/home/page.tsx:653–659`): *"THE FIRST SCREEN A REAL
USER SEES, and it used to be a title, one sentence and NOTHING ELSE… Every fixture person
already had something, so nobody had ever rendered it."* The guard test `r42`
(`scripts/render-tests.mjs:670–678`) asserts ≥3 links exist — not that any leads where the
user came for.

Worth noting the other empty states are genuinely good — `app/trials/page.tsx:182`
(*"An empty week is honest"*) is the standard. This one is the outlier.

## Layers: where depth exists, and where it is one flat list

**Real depth — three places, and only three:**

1. **The guardian consent timeline** (`app/g/controls/[childId]/page.tsx:91–127, 255–270`) —
   *"Everything that's happened"*, 38 mapped event types in parent-readable English. This is
   the deepest layer in the product: state, history, and something underneath the surface you
   can go into.
2. **RegisterReaders** — who opened your child's record, named, dated, with an off-switch.
3. **The TD register drill-down** — `/home → /club/register → /club/register/cv/{id} →
   /club/invite/{id}`. Four levels, the deepest path in the product, and it belongs to the
   paying seat. Correct priority.

**Flat everywhere else.** Nav breadth by seat: **parent 3 tabs**, coach 3–4, player 3–4, TD 8,
admin 7. Almost every leaf is home → screen → end with a single back link: `/send/[recordId]`
has exactly one outbound href (`/home`); `/club/page-edit` and `/coach/edit` are terminal. The
CV builder is a flat 3-tab strip (`BuildForm.tsx:80–84`), not a hierarchy — there is no
sequence, no step count, no next.

Two honest observations on this. The parent — the person the whole architecture exists to
protect — has the shallowest product in it: three tabs. And the consent timeline, our best
layer, is **an audit trail of paperwork**: it records emails, approvals, links and switches.
Not one of its 38 lines says anything about the child's football.

## Interactivity: what can a person actually DO?

Counted: **three** distinct interactive behaviours in the signed-in product (§1), against 99
POST-and-redirect forms and ~20 pages that are a list of things with buttons on them.

Where the product genuinely responds to you:

- **The CV builder** (`BuildForm.tsx`) — position chips that toggle *with ordinal numbering*
  (tap GK, it becomes "1 GK"), stat labels you toggle to choose what surfaces, a progress bar
  that moves as you type, a photo picker that auto-submits. This is the best-built screen in
  the app and it is the only one that feels like a tool.
- **The trials board** — faceted chips with live counts (U13 · 2, GK · 2).
- **The CV on arrival** — stat tiles count up like a scoreboard, staggered, honouring
  `prefers-reduced-motion`; the position map's primary dot pings.

Where it only accepts input: everywhere else. No drag-and-drop in the product (all seven
pointer-drag handlers are in the marketing page). No live preview — the builder's "Preview" is
an `<a href>` to a separate page (`BuildForm.tsx:59`). No optimistic UI, no pending state, no
chart, no sparkline, no diff, no version list.

## The one thing

**Today it is the public player CV, and specifically the moment it loads.** A bird's-eye pitch
inside the hero with a dot on each position the player picked, the primary one pinging
(`PlayerCV.tsx:57–74` — the code calls it *"the twist"*); the squad number as a huge ghosted
numeral behind the name; the stat tiles counting up; "Parent-approved" and "Self-reported"
stated without apology. Then, at the bottom, the constraint turned into the product's best
piece of writing:

> "There is no way to reply to a family through Pitch. […] If you want Deniz at a trial, post
> it on Pitch: families register their interest from your trial, and that is where you can
> invite them. It goes to Deniz and their parent together, and a record is kept."

**Is it the one we would choose? Yes.** It is the right screen — the charter already says so,
and it earns it. Three problems with it being the *only* one:

1. It is the screen the player **cannot see as a stranger sees it**, except through a preview
   banner.
2. The payoff is structurally withheld. `app/build/[recordId]/ready/page.tsx` is a real,
   deliberately designed moment — its own comment says *"finishing a page is the emotional high
   point of this product and it used to pass in silence."* But the token is minted at dispatch
   (D-80), so a player who completes all six steps and has not sent anything lands on **"Your
   page is ready"** with a link they cannot see, copy or show anyone. The high point requires a
   second, separate act on a terminal screen whose only exit is `/home`.
3. **That screen is not in the review set.** There is no `*_ready.png` in
   `docs/design/screens/` — the harness never captured it. The product's designed emotional
   peak has probably never been looked at.

## Where the constraints are an advantage — what is open and what is closed

The constraint is not negotiable and most of this section is about respecting it properly.

**Closed. Do not propose these, and I am not:** feed, likes, follows, DMs, comments, a
recommender, any discovery surface for a U16, leaderboards, and — explicitly, from the brief —
*"notifications to minors are strictly functional: no streaks, no re-engagement prompts,
ever."* That rules out every standard retention mechanic. Correctly.

**Open, and entirely unused.** Here is the analytical point I would put in front of BUZ:

> **The constraint closes the social loop. It does not close the temporal one. We have built as
> though it closed both.**

Everything that makes a product feel alive is either *other people reacting to you* (closed) or
*your own life changing over time* (wide open, and we have already built the schema for it).

Five moves that are open right now and break nothing:

1. **Time.** Seasons, "since", then-and-now. `record_entry` exists with six types and renders
   nowhere. A player seeing their own history is not a social feature and no rule forbids it.
   This is the single biggest unused axis in the product.
2. **Consequence we already log.** RegisterReaders and the 38-event consent log prove the
   product can say *"something happened because of you."* That is a return reason with no feed
   and no graph. It needs a home, not an invention.
3. **The coach's voice.** `coach_note` and `competency_observation` are in the schema. A note
   from your own coach at your own club is already inside the permission model — it is not
   contact from a stranger. It is December's work; it is also the only thing that makes the
   record *living*.
4. **Data rendered as data.** 108 SVGs, none plotting anything. The shape of a season is not a
   social feature.
5. **The club's calendar.** Trials, squads, seasons are inherently time-based and entirely
   non-social. The TD home already shows "COMING UP".

What the constraint genuinely buys us, and we under-sell: a chronological, scoped,
non-recommended product is **calm**, and calm is a real differentiator to a parent who has
spent five years being farmed by junior sport apps. `/jobs` says *"Nothing here is ranked or
recommended"* and `/trials` says *"An empty week is honest."* Those lines are an asset. They
appear twice.
